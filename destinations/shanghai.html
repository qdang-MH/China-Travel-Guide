小笼包 · Xiǎolóngbāo

Xiaolongbao

Photo source and license →

Thin-skinned steamed dumplings filled with pork and hot broth.

¥25–¥60 Spice: None

Where: Jia Jia Tang Bao

How to eat: Lift gently onto a spoon, bite a small opening, sip the broth, then finish the dumpling.

生煎包 · Shēngjiān Bāo

Shengjian Bao

Photo source and license →

Pan-fried buns with crisp bottoms, soft tops, and juicy pork filling.

¥15–¥35 Spice: None

Where: Yang's Dumplings or a trusted local shop

How to eat: Let them cool briefly; the filling may be very hot.

葱油拌面 · Cōngyóu Bànmiàn

Scallion-Oil Noodles

Photo source and license →

Simple noodles coated with fragrant scallion oil and soy sauce.

¥20–¥45 Spice: None

Where: Local noodle shop

How to eat: Ideal as a light lunch or as an additional shared dish.

红烧肉 · Hóngshāo Ròu

Red-Braised Pork

Photo source and license →

Rich pork belly slowly braised with soy, sugar, and aromatics.

¥60–¥120 Spice: None

Where: Shanghainese family-style restaurant

How to eat: Order one portion for the table and balance it with vegetables.

Restaurant Profiles

Three practical choices

佳家汤包

Jia Jia Tang Bao

Best for: Classic soup dumplings

A strong first introduction to Shanghai-style xiaolongbao.

  • Typical cost: ¥60–¥100/person
  • Reservations: No reservations; expect a queue
  • Ordering guide: For five: 3–4 baskets of dumplings, one noodle dish, one vegetable dish, and drinks.
Amap Google Maps

南翔馒头店

Nanxiang Mantou Dian

Best for: Yu Garden convenience

Fits naturally into the Yu Garden visit and offers classic dumplings.

  • Typical cost: ¥60–¥120/person
  • Reservations: Go early to reduce waiting
  • Ordering guide: Choose one classic basket, one crab-roe basket, and shared side dishes.
Amap Google Maps

鼎泰丰

Din Tai Fung (IAPM)

Best for: Comfortable and predictable

A reliable, comfortable option near the French Concession.

  • Typical cost: ¥120–¥220/person
  • Reservations: Helpful at peak times
  • Ordering guide: Dumplings, greens, fried rice or noodles, and tea.
